Turn YouTube Into an Interactive Learning App — Free

The traditional online learning model has a fundamental problem: passive consumption. You watch a video, understand it in the moment, and forget most of it within days. The Ebbinghaus forgetting curve tells us that without active retrieval practice, 70% of new information is lost within 24 hours. Coursera, Udemy, and Pluralsight built subscription businesses on top of this problem — the churn rate is high because the retention rate is low.

PDFreeto's AI Learning tool does something different. Paste any YouTube URL and we extract the transcript, send it to AI, and generate a complete interactive learning app — with quizzes that test you on the actual content, flashcards built from the key concepts, and a tutor that answers your questions in plain language. The whole process takes about 60 seconds.

Why NotebookLM works for this

The pipeline uses YouTube's auto-generated captions (available on most videos) to extract a full text transcript. This transcript becomes the source material for the AI, which then structures it into a complete learning experience. For a five-hour course, the AI reads everything and condenses it into an interactive app you can use in minutes.

What the generated app includes

Every generated app includes: a structured overview of the main topics covered in the video, plain-language explanations of each concept, a quiz with questions drawn directly from the content (not generic questions), flashcards for key terms and ideas, and a chat interface where you can ask follow-up questions about anything in the video. The app runs entirely in your browser — no login, no backend, no data stored.

What kinds of videos work best

Any YouTube video with captions works — university lectures, technical tutorials, official product courses, long-form educational content. Videos with natural structure (chapters, numbered steps, clear topic transitions) produce better-organised apps. Short videos under 5 minutes may not have enough content for a full quiz. Very long playlists are truncated to the first 40,000 characters of transcript, which covers roughly 2–3 hours of content.

Limitations

The tool requires YouTube auto-captions or manual subtitles to be enabled on the video. Music videos, videos with no speech, and videos where the creator has disabled captions will not work. The generated app is a static HTML file — it works in your browser session and can be downloaded and run locally, but it is not permanently hosted anywhere. For very specialised or technical content, the AI may occasionally simplify explanations — the tool is designed for learning, not for reference-level accuracy.
